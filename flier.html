You are being
followed.

That black camera on the pole at the end of your street is an automated license plate reader. Flock Safety makes most of them. Here is what it does, who it answers to, and what a year of audits and court records turned up.

It logs every car. Yours too.

It photographs every vehicle that passes. Plate, state, make, model, color, plus fingerprint details like bumper stickers, dents, and bike racks. Each one stamped with the exact time and place and uploaded to a searchable database. Flock's network runs more than 20 billion scans a month across thousands of communities. One scan is nothing. Thousands of them map where you live, work, and worship, who you visit, and which doctor you see. No warrant required.

Sources: Flock Safety · ACLU "Get the Flock Out" · EFF

It gets the wrong people pulled over.

A camera misreads one character and the system tells an officer your car is stolen. In 2026 the LAPD Inspector General reviewed 498 stolen-vehicle alerts and found 161 of them false, a 32.3% error rate. Mostly not bad reads either. The hotlists themselves were stale: recovered cars never cleared, outdated theft reports, uncorrected data entry. LA let its Flock contract expire over it. Nobody catches this without an audit, and almost nobody audits.

Sources: LAPD Office of the Inspector General · 404 Media · Institute for Justice

The access is the real problem.

In August 2026 the Washington Post documented at least 50 officers charged with or accused of using plate readers to track women they knew, with Flock's system involved in 46 of those cases. Closer to home, an IMPD officer ran the system hundreds of times for no legitimate purpose. He was suspended and the chief recommended firing him. In Milwaukee, a victim only found out by looking herself up on a public log site. That is the pattern. Victims find it, not internal audits.

Sources: Washington Post · IMPD · Milwaukee County charging documents

And the whole thing is wide open.

In December 2025 researchers found 60+ Flock cameras live-streaming to the open internet with no password. Anyone could watch, pull a month of archived video, even change settings. One pointed at a playground. A researcher rooted a camera by pressing a button and plugging in over USB. Flock customer logins have turned up for sale on Russian cybercrime forums, and the company did not require two-factor login until August 2026.

Sources: 404 Media · Benn Jordan / GainSec (51 findings, 22 CVEs) · Sen. Wyden letter to the FTC

48%
Atlanta has more Flock cameras than any city in America, over 5,000. Per its own numbers reported to the FBI, homicide clearance fell from 53.4% in 2021 to 48% in 2025, its worst of the period. Auto theft, the crime these should be best at, still clears near one in ten. Source: Atlanta Community Press Collective / FBI Crime Data Explorer
