108 cameras. Six agencies. No local rules.

License plate readers photograph every passing car in this county and log the plate, the place, and the time in a database searchable far beyond Indiana. This site tracks what each town has actually put in writing, and what it has not.

The short version 30 seconds

These cameras record every car, not just suspect vehicles. Indiana law sets no limits on how long that data is kept, who it is shared with, or who audits the searches, so every safeguard here is voluntary and can change without a vote. Of the four towns asked, one has a written policy. The ask is simple: end the contracts, or bind them with rules the public can read.

What changed in August 2026 and why it matters locally

Flock Safety spent the summer under national scrutiny. A Washington Post investigation published August 3 documented at least 50 cases in which officers were charged with or accused of misusing plate reader systems to surveil partners, former partners, or women they wanted to meet. Flock's system was involved in 46 of them. On August 13 the company announced changes: default retention cut from 30 days to 7, required case codes on searches, automatic lockouts for abnormal search behavior, mandatory audit review by the end of 2026, and multi-factor authentication.

Read the fine print and the local question writes itself. These are defaults and customer obligations, and their effectiveness depends on the agencies themselves setting up and enforcing them. A default can be changed back by any customer who wants a longer window. An audit requirement means nothing if nobody outside the department ever reads the audit.

The open question for every agency on this page: which of those settings have you adopted, who reviews your search logs, and where can a resident read the answer? As of this writing, no Hendricks County agency has published one.

What to ask for in order of preference

  1. Do not renew the contract. Bloomington let its contract expire in March 2026. Towns from Rhode Island to Arizona have done the same this year. The county functioned before these cameras and will function after.
  2. Failing that, adopt binding public rules. A policy voted on by the council, not a vendor webpage. Independent review of search logs. A published answer on which of Flock's August settings the department has actually turned on.
  3. At minimum, no quiet renewals. Cost, term, camera count, and retention discussed in the open. Two of these contracts renew automatically unless somebody gives notice, which means doing nothing is itself a decision to continue.
