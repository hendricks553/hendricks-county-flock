108 cameras. Six agencies. One written policy.

License plate readers photograph every passing car in this county and log the plate, the place, and the time in a database searchable far beyond Indiana. This site collects what each town has put in writing, so residents can read it for themselves.

The short version 30 seconds

These cameras record every car, not just suspect vehicles. Indiana law sets no limits on how long that data is kept, who it is shared with, or who audits the searches, so every safeguard here is voluntary and can change without a vote. Of the four towns asked, one has produced a written policy. The ask is simple: end the contracts, or set rules the public can read.

What changed in August 2026 and why it matters locally

Flock Safety spent the summer under national scrutiny. A Washington Post investigation published August 3 documented at least 50 cases in which officers were charged with or accused of misusing plate reader systems to surveil partners, former partners, or women they wanted to meet. Flock's system was involved in 46 of them. On August 13 the company announced changes: default retention cut from 30 days to 7, required case codes on searches, automatic lockouts for abnormal search behavior, mandatory audit review by the end of 2026, and multi-factor authentication.

These are real improvements, and worth acknowledging as such. They are also defaults and customer obligations, which means their effect depends on what each agency configures and enforces. A default retention window can be set back by any customer. An audit requirement works to the extent somebody actually reviews the audit.

Which makes for a straightforward local question: which of those settings has each agency adopted, what is the retention window today, and who reviews the search logs? None of that is published for any Hendricks County agency yet, and none of it would be difficult to answer.

What to ask for in order of preference

  1. Do not renew the contract. Bloomington let its contract expire in March 2026, and reporting this year puts cancellations or rejections across roughly 28 states. The county functioned before these cameras and would function after.
  2. If that can't happen quickly, three concrete limits. Formal limits on who may search the database and on what basis. Sharing restricted to our own department. Renewals brought to a public agenda with time before the notice deadline, so the council is making a choice rather than letting one pass.
  3. And answer the settings question. Flock changed several defaults in August 2026. What is the retention window here today, are searches tied to case numbers, and who reviews the audit logs? None of that is published for any agency in this county, and none of it is hard to answer.
