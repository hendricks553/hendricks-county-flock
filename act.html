Pick one thing. Any one helps.

Most of this record was built by a handful of people filing forms and reading what came back. None of it took expertise, money, or a group.

File a records request 15 minutes, no cost

Any person can request a town's plate reader contracts, payments, and use policy under Indiana's Access to Public Records Act. You do not have to live in the town. You do not have to explain yourself beyond the request itself.

Paste this

I'd like copies of: (1) any contracts or agreements with Flock Safety or any other automated license plate reader vendor, including the original agreement and any renewals or amendments; (2) any invoices, purchase orders, or payment records under those agreements; and (3) any policy, ordinance, or written guidance the town or police department has adopted governing use of license plate reader cameras, including retention periods, data sharing, and audit procedures. Electronic copies by email are preferred. If any part of this request would involve a fee, please provide an estimated cost before proceeding. This request is for personal, noncommercial civic purposes.
Keep that last sentence. Danville declined a request in July 2026 on a commercial-purpose basis under a provision of the Act aimed mainly at bulk resale of records. Stating your purpose inside the request settles the question before it comes up.

The phrase "or any other automated license plate reader vendor" matters too. Plainfield turned out to hold a second contract with a different company, worth roughly $28,000 a year, which a Flock-only request would have missed entirely.

If they ignore you or deny it

Two things are worth knowing, and neither involves a lawyer.

  • The 30-day benchmark. The Act does not set a hard deadline, but Indiana's Public Access Counselor treats roughly 30 days from receipt as a reasonable response time, weighed against how big the agency and the request are. Small towns run on small staffs, and a delay is more often a backlog than a refusal.
  • The complaint is free. The Public Access Counselor takes formal complaints at in.gov/pac. The office asks the agency to respond and issues a written opinion. The opinion is advisory rather than enforceable, but it is official, public, and citable, and agencies frequently produce the records once a complaint lands.

Send what you get either way. A denial letter is a record too.

What actually lands, and what does not
  • Lead with the local document, not the national story. A town's own records carry more weight in its own chamber than a statistic from another state, and citing them is not an accusation, just a reading of the paperwork.
  • Separate the technology from the department early. Councils hear most criticism of a police program as criticism of their police. Making clear at the start that the objection is to the system and the outside access, not to local conduct, keeps the rest of the time on the substance.
  • Ask answerable questions. What is the retention window today? Who reads the audit logs? Did the council vote on this? Questions get further than charges, and a question a council cannot answer in the room tends to get answered later.
  • The hardest point to answer is the scope of access. A council can speak for its own department. It cannot speak for agencies in thirty other states, for the vendor's security practices, or for whoever has bought credentials on a criminal forum.
  • Expect the phone comparison. It comes up nearly every time and it is usually asked in good faith. The reply is on the case page.
  • Follow up in writing. Send your sources to the clerk afterward for the record. What is said out loud gets forgotten. What is emailed is a document.

The rest of it

  • Map a camera. See one that is not on deflock.me? Drop a pin. That map is where the count of 108 comes from, and it is only as complete as the people driving past have made it.
  • Check whether you have been searched. Some departments release their search logs through public records, and Have I Been Flocked aggregates what has been released. Several of the misuse cases reported in 2026 surfaced because someone looked themselves up there.
  • Email your state legislators. Indiana's zero-rules status is the root of every open question on this site, and only the General Assembly can close it. Find yours at iga.in.gov. Offices track how many constituents from each community write in.
  • Sign the petition. Eyes Off Indiana runs the statewide one.
  • Send documents. Received records from any Indiana agency? They get posted here and passed to Eyes Off Indiana's public archive. hendricks553@proton.me
  • Share this page. That is the whole ask.
