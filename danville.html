The denial

A request for Danville's Flock contract, payment records, and use policy was denied in July 2026. The stated basis was a provision of Indiana's Access to Public Records Act that lets an agency decline records it believes will be used for commercial purposes.

The requester had already stated in writing that the records were for personal, non-commercial civic use, and confirmed it again after the denial. The request was then forwarded from the clerk-treasurer's office to the police department on July 27, 2026. Nothing has come back since.

The Public Access Counselor's working benchmark for a reasonable response is 30 days from receipt, weighed against the size of the agency and the request. This request covers three cameras.

Why the commercial-purpose basis matters to you

The provision exists to stop bulk resale of records like property and licensing data. Applying it to a resident asking about surveillance cameras sets a presumption that anyone requesting these documents intends to sell them.

The practical lesson for anyone filing in Danville or anywhere else: state your purpose in the request itself. One sentence removes the basis before it can be used. The request template on this site already includes it.

What is known anyway

Danville's own records are unavailable, but Plainfield publishes its sharing lists, and Danville appears on them. That establishes the town's cameras are part of Flock's sharing network rather than a closed local system, and that plate reads collected here are reachable by agencies outside Indiana.

Three cameras is a small deployment. That cuts both ways: it is a modest thing to end, and a modest thing to write a policy for.

Where to raise it

Danville Town Council meets the 1st and 3rd Wednesday of the month at 7 PM, Town Hall, 49 N. Wayne St.

Public comment is confirmed: fill out the request-to-speak form at the door before 7 PM. Three minutes per speaker.

Worth asking

  • What does the town pay for its license plate readers, and out of which fund?
  • Is there a written use policy, and did the council adopt it?
  • Why was a resident's records request treated as commercial after he said in writing that it was not?
  • The request was referred to the police department on July 27. When will it be answered?
